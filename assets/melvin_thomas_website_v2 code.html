About

Precision chemistry.
A clear line to impact.

I'm a synthetic organic chemist working at the intersection of precision and scale. For 7 years, I've taken hard synthetic problems and turned them into characterized, reproducible compounds — the kind that move pipelines forward.

  • At Bio-Rad, I synthesize and characterize novel fluorescent dyes for biological imaging — advancing reagent pipelines from concept to compound-ready stage with NMR, HPLC, FT-IR, and GC-MS validating every batch to >95% purity.
  • At UCSB, I engineered a heterogeneous catalyst and continuous flow reactor system to synthesize Moclobemide more cleanly and scalably than conventional batch routes — and developed two solvent-free green chemistry protocols that are the foundation of upcoming publications.
  • Across bioactive alkaloid analogs, natural product isolation, chiral aziridine synthesis, and industry-partnered flow chemistry — I've built the kind of range that lets me walk into an unfamiliar synthetic problem and find a path through it.

Beyond the Bench

The Creative Mind
Behind the Chemistry

The same instincts that make a good chemist — pattern recognition, tolerance for ambiguity, the ability to reframe a problem entirely — show up in creative work too. I write, direct, and compose. Not as a hobby separate from science, but as a practice that sharpens the way I think across all domains.

🎬
Storytelling → Science Communication
Directing a national award-winning science film taught me how to translate dense technical content into narratives that move people. That skill is directly at work when I present research findings, write cross-functional briefs, or align a biology team with a synthetic strategy.
✍️
Poetry → Precision of Language
Writing poetry is an exercise in saying exactly the right thing with the minimum number of words — the same discipline that makes a well-characterized compound report or a clear methods section. Clarity is clarity, whether the medium is verse or a reaction scheme.
🎵
Music → Systems Thinking
Composing requires holding an entire architecture in your head while attending to individual notes — the same cognitive mode as designing a multi-step synthetic route. Both demand awareness of how each decision propagates through the whole.
🔬
Multidisciplinary by Design
Chemists who only think like chemists hit walls that require other tools to get through. My creative practice keeps me genuinely cross-disciplinary — comfortable moving between intuitive and analytical modes, and less likely to be stuck in a single problem-solving frame.
